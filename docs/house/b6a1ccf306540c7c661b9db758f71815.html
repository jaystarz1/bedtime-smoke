The Softest Thing in the Wood

The day had gone quiet in Hush Hollow, and the Wood path was cool underfoot. Hedgehog and Pup walked along it slowly. There was nowhere to be but here.

At the bend, where the tall grass grows, the wind had left something behind. Thistledown. A whole drift of it, white and puffed and piled as high as their knees.

Hedgehog picked up a handful. It weighed nothing at all.

"This is the softest thing in the Wood," said Pup. "There is enough here for every bed in Hush Hollow."

"Then we will take it round," said Hedgehog. "Slowly. A little for you, a little for me."

So they found a wide curl of birch bark, and they filled it together. Pup held the bark steady. Hedgehog set the thistledown in, and patted it down, and set in a little more.

A little more, and a little more.

Nobody hurried. Nobody reached past the other. The dark came down between the branches, kind and slow, and the small stars came out over the Wood.

Then down the big tree with the knot-shaped window came Mrs. Squirrel, her tail curled high behind her, with three small ones tumbling after.

"My goodness," she said. "What a lot of soft for two."

The three small ones looked at the bark. They looked and looked, and said not a word.

"It is not for two," said Pup. "A little for you, a little for me."

So they went round the Wood and gave it out, slow and steady, a handful at a time.

Some went up to Mrs. Squirrel's nook, for the three small ones to sleep on. Some went into the mouse hole under the root. Some went round the rim of Robin's nest in the hawthorn. Some went into the crack in the old wall where the wren sleeps.

A little for you, a little for me.

And when the birch bark was almost bare, one last handful lay in the bottom of it. Hedgehog took half. Pup took half. It was a small thing, and it was plenty.

Then home along the Wood path, slow, slow, with the bark light as a leaf between them.

They tucked their thistledown into their own two beds and lay down on it, tired and glad, with the whole quiet Wood around them.

Hedgehog yawned. Pup yawned right after.

"A little for you," said Pup, sleepy now.

"A little for me," said Hedgehog.

And all through the Wood, every small one lay on something soft.

Goodnight, Hedgehog. Goodnight, Pup. Goodnight, Mrs. Squirrel, and her three small ones. Goodnight, Moon, up there over the Wood. Goodnight, Hush Hollow.
