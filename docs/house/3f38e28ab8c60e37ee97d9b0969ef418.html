Little by Little

Here is the Wood. The path is soft. The light is low.

Here is Mouse, with a small blue cup of tea. Mrs. Squirrel poured it, full to the brim, and her tail curled around the pot to keep it warm. "Sit awhile," she says. "Someone is out there in the ferns."

Mouse sits. Mouse waits.

At the edge of the lantern light, a fern moves. Then it is still. Then it moves again.

"Hello," says Mouse. "I have tea."

Nothing. That is all right. Mouse holds the cup and hums, and the Wood hums back. Little by little, little by little.

Here is a leaf. Here is a stone. Here is a slow brown beetle going home. Mouse names each one, quiet as a blanket. The ferns lean in to listen.

Then a hand. Just one, at the edge of the green. Then two eyes, round and shy.

"That is Bunny," says Mrs. Squirrel, low and soft. "Bunny is new here."

Mouse does not hurry. Mouse sets the second cup down in the moss, halfway between them, and sits back.

Little by little, little by little.

Bunny comes one step. The tea steams. Bunny comes another step. The lantern glows. Bunny sits down in the moss and holds the little cup in both hands, and there they are, side by side, two shapes in one small light.

"Hello," says Bunny.

"Hello," says Mouse.

That is all. That is enough.

They drink their tea. Mrs. Squirrel sets out three biscuits and takes one for herself, whiskers twitching, tail tucked warm. The Wood goes quiet. The crickets slow. The lantern dims to a soft orange coin.

Little by little, little by little.

Now the ferns are folded. Now the path is grey. Now Mouse and Bunny walk home together, side by side, slow and slow and slow.

Here is the door. Here is the bed. Here is the blanket, pulled up warm.

Goodnight, small blue cup. Goodnight, lantern. Goodnight, ferns and moss and slow brown beetle.

Goodnight, Mrs. Squirrel, and thank you for the tea.

Goodnight, Bunny. Goodnight, Mouse.

Goodnight, Moon.

Goodnight, Hush Hollow. Sleep now. Sleep.
