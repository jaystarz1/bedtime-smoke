Little by Little

In Hush Hollow the evening came slow and blue, with a hill and a hush and a star or two.

Pup went up where the long grass grows, where the wind lies down and the clover blows. And there at the edge, where the tall ferns hide, someone was waiting on the other side.

Fox stood still where the soft shadows fall, and said not a word, not a word at all.

"Hello," said Pup, and sat on the ground. "I'll wait right here. I don't mind the sound of the wind in the grass and the hush of the hill." And Pup waited, gentle. And Fox waited, still.

Little by little, little by little, a friend comes near. Little by little, little by little, there is nothing to fear.

Mr. Crow came down with his glossy black wing, and he ruffled his feathers and started to sing a low sleepy song for the top of the hill, and Fox took one step. Then another. Then still.

Pup held out a clover with a soft white crown. Fox reached, and took it, and sat slowly down. No hurry, no rush, on the grass side by side, with the long evening going out like the tide.

Little by little, little by little, a friend comes near. Little by little, little by little, there is nothing to fear.

They spoke of small things as the light went low, of clover, and hilltops, and Mr. Crow. And Mr. Crow rustled and said, soft and slow, "That is how friendships in the Hollow grow."

Then down the hill, where the grass had gone grey, and the two of them wandered the warm way, past the willow, the window, the low garden gate, where the lamps of the Hollow were staying up late.

Little by little, little by little, a friend comes near. Little by little, little by little, there is nothing to fear.

The stars took their places. The Moon came to keep her watch on the Hollow, so quiet, so deep.

Goodnight, Pup. Goodnight, Fox. Goodnight, Mr. Crow in the tree. Goodnight to the hill and the clover and grass. Goodnight, Hush Hollow. Goodnight from me.

The hill is asleep in its blanket of night. Now Pup, now Fox, it is time. Goodnight.
