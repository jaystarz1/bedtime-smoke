Goodnight, Little Hill

Here is the Hollow. Here is the hill. The grass is soft. The light is low.

Here is Mouse, sitting on the hill. Here is Bunny, sitting beside Mouse. They sit side by side and watch the sky go dim.

In the grass, something small and round. A berry. Then two. Then a whole little patch of them, dark and sweet and cool.

"Berries," says Mouse.

"Berries," says Bunny.

They pick them one by one into a leaf, until the leaf is full.

Mr. Crow comes down from the old pine. His black feathers shine. He tucks his wings and looks at the leaf, and he does not say a word.

So Mouse holds out a berry.

"A little for you, a little for me."

Mr. Crow takes it in his beak and bobs his head, and he ruffles all over, pleased.

Then the beetles come, slow along the stem. Then a small brown bird. Then a sleepy bee with dust on her legs.

And Bunny gives them each one.

"A little for you, a little for me."

One berry for the beetle. One for the brown bird. One for the bee. One for Mr. Crow again, because he waited so nicely. One for Mouse. One for Bunny.

The leaf grows light. Everyone has a piece. Nobody goes without.

Mouse licks a purple thumb. Bunny licks a purple thumb. They laugh a small laugh, the kind that comes at the end of a day.

"A little for you, a little for me."

Then down the hill they go, slow and soft, home to the Hollow.

The lanterns are warm. The beds are ready. Mr. Crow settles high in the old pine and folds himself up like a black leaf.

And now the round of goodnights.

Goodnight, hill. Goodnight, grass. Goodnight, berry patch in the dark, keeping the rest for tomorrow.

Goodnight, beetle. Goodnight, brown bird. Goodnight, sleepy bee.

Goodnight, Mr. Crow, high in the pine.

Goodnight, moon. Goodnight, stars keeping watch.

Goodnight, Mouse. Goodnight, Bunny.

The room is warm. The room is still.

Goodnight, Hush Hollow.
