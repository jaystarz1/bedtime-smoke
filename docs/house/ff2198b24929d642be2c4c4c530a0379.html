The Warm Place to Rest

Evening came slow to the Meadow in Hush Hollow. The clover folded its small leaves. The tall grass swayed, soft and gold, then soft and blue.

Bunny and Hedgehog sat on a woolly blanket at the edge of the clover. Down in the grass, the first fireflies came up, one little glow, then two.

But Robin was not ready for night.

She fluttered to a fern. She fluttered to a mossy stone. She fluttered to a tall stem of grass, and it bobbed and swayed beneath her. Her feathers stood up, all ruffled.

"Oh, the day is going," said Robin, in a small, sad peep. "I love the day so much. I do not want it to go."

Bunny got up. Hedgehog got up too, a little behind, quiet as the grass, and brought the woolly blanket along.

They did not hurry. They crossed the clover, step by step, and sat down right beside Robin's stem.

"We are here," said Bunny, softly.

Hedgehog did not say much, most days. Tonight Hedgehog said a little. "The day is only resting, Robin. It will come back in the morning."

Then Bunny held out one hand, open and still.

Robin tilted her head. She hopped from her stem onto Bunny's hand. It was warm there, warm as a stone in the sun.

"You are safe, you are safe," said Bunny.

Hedgehog lifted a corner of the woolly blanket and tucked it around Bunny's hand, so Robin sat snug in a woolly ring.

Robin gave one little shiver, and then no shiver at all. She fluffed her feathers. She tucked one wing.

"You are safe, you are safe," whispered Hedgehog.

The three of them sat together while the Meadow went quiet. The fireflies drifted up out of the clover, one and one and one, each a small gold glow. A cricket sang a sleepy song. The sky went deep and still.

"The day is only resting," said Robin, sleepy now. "Only resting."

She gave a long, soft trill. Then she flew up, low and easy, to her nest in the hazel at the edge of the Meadow, and tucked her head beneath her wing.

Bunny's hand stayed warm where Robin had rested. So did Hedgehog's corner of the blanket.

Bunny and Hedgehog spread the woolly blanket back on the clover and lay down, side by side. The grass was soft beneath them. The fireflies glowed low and slow. Bunny yawned. Hedgehog yawned.

"You are safe, you are safe," Hedgehog whispered, to Bunny, to Robin, to the whole sleepy Meadow.

Goodnight, Robin, snug in your nest. Goodnight, Bunny. Goodnight, Hedgehog. Goodnight, fireflies, low in the clover. Goodnight, Moon. Goodnight, Hush Hollow.
