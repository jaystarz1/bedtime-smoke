Goodnight, Hush Hollow

The sun went down slow over Hush Hollow. Up on the Hill, the grass was warm and the wind was small. Fox and Mouse walked up the path together, with one folded quilt between them.

At the top of the Hill sat Mr. Crow, blinking and blinking, his feathers all rumpled, his wings folded wrong.

"I am too sleepy to get ready for sleep," said Mr. Crow.

"Then we will help," said Fox.

"Hush now, snug now," said Mouse.

Mouse smoothed the nest of grass until it was round and deep. Fox set the lantern down low and turned the little light down small.

And the lantern is asleep on the soft, soft Hill.

Fox brought a cup of warm water. Mr. Crow drank it in three slow sips and gave a long, croaky yawn.

"Hush now, snug now," said Fox.

Mouse folded the quilt in half, and then in half again, and laid it over Mr. Crow's rumpled feathers, right up under his beak.

And the quilt is asleep, and the lantern is asleep on the soft, soft Hill.

Then came the last bedtime step, the one Mr. Crow liked best of all. Fox sang one line. Mouse sang the same line after. Low and slow, low and slow, the way you would want it sung to you.

Mr. Crow's eyes went shut. His beak went quiet. Mr. Crow's one black wing settled down soft.

And Mr. Crow is asleep, and the quilt is asleep, and the lantern is asleep on the soft, soft Hill.

"Hush now, snug now," said Fox and Mouse together, very quietly, the way you talk when someone is already dreaming.

Then down the Hill they went, side by side, back through the Hollow to their own two beds. Mouse pulled the covers up. Fox pulled the covers up. The Moon came out over Hush Hollow, quiet and kind, and kept the light for everyone.

And Mouse is asleep, and Fox is asleep, and Mr. Crow is asleep, and the whole warm Hollow is asleep on the soft, soft Hill.

Hush now. Snug now.

Goodnight, Fox. Goodnight, Mouse. Goodnight, Mr. Crow. Goodnight, Moon.

Goodnight, Hush Hollow.
