Just You and Me, Soft and Slow

In Hush Hollow, the evening came down soft as a blanket.

Bunny sat on the doorstep with two cups of warm milk. One for Bunny. One for a friend.

"Hedgehog," Bunny called, gentle as anything. "Come sit by the stream with me."

So they went, side by side, down the path to the water. No hurry. No plan. Just two friends and a long slow evening.

"What shall we do?" said Hedgehog.

"Nothing at all," said Bunny. "Just you and me, soft and slow."

The stream sang its small song over the stones. Hush, hush, hush.

They settled on the mossy bank with the cups warm in their hands. The reeds swayed. The water carried rings of light along, one after another, all the way around the bend.

Mr. Trout came up from the deep pool, slow as a shadow, his silver side turning in the dark water. He tipped one fin at them, the way he always did, and drifted back down to his cool bed of stones.

"Goodnight, Mr. Trout," said Hedgehog.

Bunny leaned nearer, shoulder against shoulder. They hummed a song with no words in it, the kind that comes out on its own. And the stream hummed too. Hush, hush, hush.

Just you and me, soft and slow.

The dew came down and beaded on the grass, tiny and bright. Hedgehog gathered three drops in one hand and gave them to Bunny, and Bunny gave them back, and that was the whole game. It was not much of a game at all. The whole evening was warmer for it.

Then it was time. They went along the path home, slow and easy, the cups empty now, the Hollow quiet all around them.

"Tomorrow?" said Hedgehog.

"Tomorrow," said Bunny. "Just you and me, soft and slow."

At the door they said their goodnights, the way friends do, and the warm thing they had made together stayed on, humming quietly in the dark.

Goodnight, Bunny. Goodnight, Hedgehog. Goodnight, Mr. Trout, asleep in the deep cool water. Goodnight, stream. Goodnight, moon. Goodnight, Hush Hollow.

Sleep now, soft and slow.
