The Warm Way Home

In Hush Hollow, when the day grows soft, the light goes gold and slow.

Bunny and Hedgehog sat in the garden, side by side on the old flat stone. The beans had been picked. The watering can was empty. The garden was doing what gardens do at the end of a day, which is nothing at all, very slowly.

Dandelion came through the row of peas, tail up, whiskers twitching, the way she always came.

"Time," she said.

"One more minute," said Bunny.

"One more minute," said Dandelion, and she sat down beside them and let the minute be long.

The garden went quiet. A cricket started up under the beans. Somewhere a bird settled in the hedge and stopped talking about the day.

Hedgehog leaned into Dandelion's warm side.

"Are you here?" said Hedgehog, though Hedgehog knew.

"Here," said Dandelion. "Loved and safe, loved and safe."

They went up the garden path together, slow as slow. Bunny carried the empty can. Hedgehog carried a bean pod, because a bean pod is worth carrying. Dandelion carried nothing but the two of them, one hand each.

Inside the den, the blankets were already turned down. That is a small thing that somebody does when nobody is watching, and it makes a bed feel like being expected.

Dandelion tucked the blanket under Bunny's chin and gave it the little tug that means stay. Then Hedgehog's. Then the tug again, just to be sure.

She hummed. It was not much of a song. It went up a bit and came down a bit and mostly stayed in the middle, the way the songs do that you have heard a thousand times and never once listened to.

"Loved and safe," she hummed. "Loved and safe."

Out the window, the Moon kept her quiet watch over the Hollow, and the stars came out one and one and one.

Bunny's eyes went heavy. Hedgehog's went heavier.

"Will you be here when it's morning?" said Bunny, from far away inside the blanket.

"I will be here," said Dandelion. "I am here when you are sleeping too. That is the part you don't have to stay awake for."

And Bunny let go of the minute, and it floated off somewhere, and did not matter.

The cricket kept on under the beans. The peas stood still in their rows. The den was warm and dim and smelled like earth and clean blankets.

Dandelion sat in the doorway a while longer, doing nothing at all, very slowly.

Loved and safe. Loved and safe.

Goodnight, Bunny. Goodnight, Hedgehog. Goodnight, Dandelion, in the doorway. Goodnight, garden, and goodnight, Moon.

Goodnight, Hush Hollow.
