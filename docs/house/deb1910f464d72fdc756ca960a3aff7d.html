Side by Side Again

In Hush Hollow, when the day goes quiet, the path runs down to the pond. The reeds lean over. The water holds the last of the light, soft and slow, soft and slow.

Pup and Fox came down to the pond with one little wooden boat between them, and a leaf for its sail.

"I'll hold it," said Pup.

"I'll hold it," said Fox.

And the boat tipped out of four hands at once and drifted away over the dark warm water, and the two of them stood on the bank with nothing at all to hold.

So Fox sat down at one end of the old log. Pup sat down at the other end. The log was long, and the space in the middle was longer.

Up through the reeds came Frog, with his wide green smile and his damp patient toes. He blinked once. He settled on his stone.

"That is a very long log," said Frog, "for two friends who like to sit side by side again."

Pup studied the grass. Fox studied the water.

Then Pup slid over. Only a little. Only one small slide along the log.

And Fox slid over too.

"I'm sorry," said Pup, quiet as a moth going by.

"I'm sorry too," said Fox, quieter still.

And that was all it took. That is nearly always all it takes: two small words, said gently, and two friends side by side again.

Frog slipped into the pond with hardly a ripple. He nudged the little boat home with the top of his head and set it in the reeds, safe for tomorrow.

Then Pup and Fox went up the path together, shoulder warm against shoulder, one boat between them and no argument left in it anywhere. Home came around them the way a blanket does. Somebody's window glowed gold. Somebody's kettle sighed.

Wherever you go, my love, and however far, there is always a way back to each other. It is a short way. It is two small words long.

Down at the pond the water went still. Up in the Hollow the lamps went low. Pup yawned. Fox yawned. And they lay down side by side again.

Goodnight, Pup. Goodnight, Fox. Goodnight, Frog, in your reeds by the water. Goodnight, Moon. Goodnight, Hush Hollow. Now close your eyes, my love. The night will keep you.
