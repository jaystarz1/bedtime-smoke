Goodnight, Hush Hollow

The Wood was going quiet. The last birds tucked their songs away, and the moss turned cool and soft underfoot.

Bunny and Hedgehog came home along the path, side by side, with one small lantern between them.

By the roots of the old beech tree, someone was sitting up who ought to have been lying down.

"Oh," said Mrs. Squirrel, and her tail curled over her toes. "It's Wren. She is far too sleepy to get ready for bed, and far too sleepy to say so."

Wren sat in a little heap of leaves, her eyes going shut and open and shut again.

Bunny knelt beside her. Hedgehog set the lantern down.

"We can help," said Bunny. "We know all the steps."

So they began, soft and sure.

Hedgehog smoothed the leaves into a bed and patted them flat, the way you pat a pillow. Bunny took the small blanket from its hook on the root and shook the day out of it.

Hush now, snug now.

They gave Wren a sip of water from a cup. They wiped a smudge of berry from her chin with the corner of a cloth. They found her little wooden bird, the one she likes to hold, and put it in her hand.

Hush now, snug now.

Mrs. Squirrel stayed on the branch above, her tail a slow soft flag, and she said nothing at all, because nothing needed saying.

Bunny drew the blanket up. Hedgehog tucked the edges under, all the way around, the way you would want your own edges tucked.

"Warm?" said Bunny.

Wren nodded with her eyes already shut.

"Sleep well," whispered Hedgehog.

They left the lantern by the roots, turned low, and went on down the path. The night was a little softer than before. Something warm sat in them both, quiet as a coal, and neither of them said one word about it.

At home the beds were waiting. Bunny climbed in. Hedgehog climbed in. The blankets came up under their chins, and the pillows were cool on one side and warm on the other.

Hush now, snug now.

Outside, the Moon kept her light over the Wood, and the stars stayed awake so nobody else had to.

Goodnight, Bunny. Goodnight, Hedgehog. Goodnight, Mrs. Squirrel, high in the beech tree. Goodnight, Wren, warm in her leaves. Goodnight, Moon. Goodnight, Hush Hollow.
