The Quiet Cove

Night comes to Hush Hollow.

Here is the path. Here is the little bridge. Here is the Cove, where the water is shallow and warm and still.

Mouse comes down to the sand. Bunny comes down beside Mouse.

Mr. Trout waits at the edge of his pool. His fins move slow. His silver back shines in the shallow water.

"My stones have slipped," says Mr. Trout. "The ring around my pool has come undone. And a trout has no hands."

Mouse counts the stones. Bunny counts them too. Eleven stones, round and grey and cool.

"We have hands," says Mouse.

"Two of us," says Bunny. "Two of us is enough."

So they begin.

Mouse lifts one side of a stone. Bunny lifts the other. They carry it to the edge of the pool and set it down. Together, together, soft and slow.

Here is the first stone. Here is the second. Here is the third, with sand still on it.

The mossy one is heavy. Mouse holds. Bunny holds. They count to three and lift, and it goes where it belongs.

Together, together, soft and slow.

Mr. Trout hums a low water hum. Small waves come in and go out. The sky above the Cove goes dim.

Here is the ninth stone. Here is the tenth. Here is the last one, the smallest, and Bunny carries that one alone while Mouse holds the gap open for it.

The ring is done. The pool is round again. The water inside it is quiet as a bowl.

"Alone, I could not," says Mr. Trout. "Two small ones did what neither could do."

Mouse sits down in the warm sand. Bunny sits beside Mouse. Their arms are tired. Their eyes are heavy. Together, together, soft and slow.

Now the going-home path. Now the little bridge. Now the Hollow, dim and warm and still.

Goodnight, eleven grey stones. Goodnight, quiet pool. Goodnight, Mr. Trout, in the shallow water. Goodnight, warm sand. Goodnight, small waves. Goodnight, Moon. Goodnight, stars. Goodnight, Mouse. Goodnight, Bunny. Goodnight, Hush Hollow.

Sleep now. The work is done, and the night is soft and slow.
