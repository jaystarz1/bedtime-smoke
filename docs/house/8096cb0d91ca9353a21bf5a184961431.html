The Little Crab of the Cove

Down in Hush Hollow, where the tide runs low, the Cove went quiet, the water lay slow. Pup had a pail and Fox had a shell, and the small waves hushed like a sleepy bell.

Then out from a stone came a whispering sound, a little green crab going round and round. "I have lost my pool," said the crab, "and the light is going away, and I'm small, and it's night."

Pup knelt down beside him. Fox held out a hand. "We will walk you back home, all the way down the sand." And they went the way that the little waves go, soft and slow, soft and slow.

Honey came down with her lantern of gold, her fur full of evening, her voice warm and old. "Follow the water," said Honey. "It knows. It remembers each pool where a small thing goes."

Past the round black rocks where the seaweed lay, past the little green boat asleep in the bay, they carried the crab where the quiet waves go, soft and slow, soft and slow.

The Cove was so still that the sand made no sound. They stopped at each pool that the low tide had found. "Not this one," the crab said. "Not this one, no." So on they went, soft and slow.

And then came a pool as round as a cup, a stone laid over it, a star lit up. "Here," said the crab. "Oh, here is my door." And down he went, and was lost no more.

Then Pup gave a yawn, and Fox did too, and over the water the first stars grew. Honey walked with them, her lantern low, soft and slow, soft and slow.

The Moon came up over Hush Hollow's trees. The tide breathed out. There was almost no breeze. Then blankets. Then pillows. Then one small light, and the whole warm Hollow said goodnight.

Goodnight, Pup. Goodnight, Fox. Goodnight, Honey. Goodnight, rocks. Goodnight, little crab, in your pool below. Goodnight, Moon. Goodnight, Hush Hollow. Sleep now, soft and slow.
