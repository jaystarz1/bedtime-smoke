The Garden That Gave Back

In Hush Hollow the evening came quiet and low, and the garden lay hushed in the dark. Fox came up the path with a basket of berries, past the gate and the old apple bark.

The rows were all sleeping. The bean poles stood tall. A lamp in a window glowed low. And Fox held the basket up under one arm, and went down the garden row, slow.

Then Mouse came along with an empty tin cup, where the berry vines lean on the stone. "If I share them, I'll have less," said Fox, holding tight. "And I want all of them for my own."

Honey the cat had a warm spot to keep, by the wall where the last sun had been. She stretched out her paws and she flicked her soft tail, and she purred at the two of them then.

"Give one," said Honey. "Just one, only one. The garden gives back what you grow." So Fox gave one berry. Just one, in the cup. And the night went on, soft and slow.

Then Mouse gave a laugh and said, "Come here and look!" by the beans where the tall shadows go, at a bush hanging heavy and dark to the ground, with more, and more, soft and slow.

They gathered together. They filled up the cup. They filled up the basket also. The branches bent down and kept giving them fruit, and more, and more, soft and slow.

They set out a handful for Honey to keep, and a handful for birds in the hedge, and still there was plenty, and still there was more, in a bowl on the low garden ledge.

Home through the grass with the cup and the rest, where the lamp in the window glowed low. Two friends and a basket still heavy and full, and more, and more, soft and slow.

The evening grew softer. The garden grew still. The wind in the grass sounded slow. Fox yawned in the doorway. Mouse leaned on the sill. And the lamplight was dimming down low.

Goodnight to Honey, curled up by the wall. Goodnight to the basket and row. Goodnight to the Moon and the garden and all. Goodnight, Hush Hollow. Sleep, soft and slow.
