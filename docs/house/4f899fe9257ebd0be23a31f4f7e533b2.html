Come Along, Come Along

The meadow in Hush Hollow was warm and low and green, and the grass lay down soft where the day had rested on it. Mouse and Bunny sat in the middle of it with a blanket spread out flat, and a little pile of berries in the middle of the blanket, and nothing at all to hurry them.

Honey the bee came humming a slow gold hum. Her wings made the smallest breeze. She settled on the corner of the blanket and stayed.

At the far edge of the meadow, where the grass grew tall, someone new was standing.

The someone new did not come any farther. They held their hands together and stood in the tall grass and waited, the way you wait when you are not sure a place is yours yet.

Mouse looked up.

"Come along," said Mouse. "Come along, come along."

Bunny moved the berries over and patted the blanket flat. There was room. There had always been room.

The someone new came slowly through the grass, one step, then another step, then a step that was a little quicker. Honey hummed her gold hum. The grass whispered and went still.

"Sit here," said Bunny, "beside me."

And the someone new sat down beside her, snug on the warm blanket, and the three of them were a little circle now instead of two.

They shared the berries. They shared the blanket. They shared the last long piece of the evening, while the light went from gold to soft grey to a quiet blue.

"Come along," Mouse said again, sleepily, as the crickets started up. "Come along, come along."

And it was a good thing to say, and a good thing to hear.

Nobody stood at the edge of the meadow anymore. Nobody stood apart. The warm feeling of it stayed in the grass and stayed on the blanket and stayed in the middle of the little circle, the way warmth stays in a stone after the sun goes down.

Honey hummed once more, low and gold and sleepy, and went home to her hive.

The meadow grew dim. The blanket grew still. Mouse yawned, and then Bunny yawned, and then the someone new yawned too, and that is how you know a day is finished.

Above them, the Moon.

"Come along," whispered the grass, soft and small. "Come along, come along."

So they went along home, all together, through the cool blue meadow to their own warm beds in the Hollow.

Goodnight, Mouse. Goodnight, Bunny. Goodnight, Honey, asleep in your hive.

Goodnight, meadow. Goodnight, moon.

Goodnight, Hush Hollow.
